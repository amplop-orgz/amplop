## Five things

1. **Money split into envelopes on arrival.** Each allowance is divided by the student's own percentages the moment it arrives, so the month is planned before the first purchase.
2. **Weekly release.** Food, Transport, Study and Everyday open in four shares, one each Monday, so week four is funded before week one begins.
3. **The contract itself deciding each card payment.** The kind of shop picks the envelope and the contract approves or declines in one transaction; the server cannot spend money the rules have not released.
4. **A trusted person who can open Emergency but cannot block it.** They can say yes sooner; if they do not answer, the money opens by itself after 48 hours.
5. **A parent who sees totals, not shops.** The parent sees what is left in each envelope and a few alerts, never where the money was spent.

## Design principle

<!-- principle:crypto-free -->

> **No new vocabulary.** Parents and students should not need new words to send or spend an allowance. So sign-in is a passkey, with no recovery phrase. The user never sees or pays a network fee. There is no wallet prompt. The token's name appears only in the small detail panel beside a transaction proof.

## What we looked at

We reviewed eight stablecoin cards launched or announced by October 2026. On their public pages, none offered purpose envelopes, timed release, or a second person who can approve.[^reviewed]
