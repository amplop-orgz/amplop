# AmplopEnvelopes self-review, October 2026

## 1. Summary

**Self-reviewed, findings published.** This is not an external audit.

Three independent passes reviewed `AmplopEnvelopes.sol`: the x-ray threat model, the solidity-auditor skill in loop mode, and a manual fresh-eyes review. The tools were Slither, Aderyn and the Foundry suite. The reviews found no way to take or lose tokens. Token accounting held on every path in every pass, and in the invariant suite at 1,000 runs of 50 calls.

They found six real issues in the access and recovery rules. All six are fixed, each with a regression test. A review of the fixes found two problems in them, and both are fixed too. Four findings are accepted with reasons, including the demo-mode clock. The analysis-tool false positives are marked inline. No high or medium finding is open.

## 4. Threat model and invariants

The full threat model is in [`x-ray/threat-model.md`](x-ray/threat-model.md), the entry points in [`x-ray/entry-points.md`](x-ray/entry-points.md), and the invariants in [`x-ray/invariants.md`](x-ray/invariants.md).

The x-ray skill classifies the contract as custodial payment escrow with vault-style internal accounting. There is no owner, admin, pause or upgrade path. Adversaries ranked by impact:

1. A compromised card-service key. It is the only role that can move tokens out (to the fixed settlement address only), on every account, with no delay.
2. A malicious or careless trusted person.
3. A student gaming their own clock on a demo deployment.
4. The AUSD issuer.
5. A compromised treasury key, which can only credit accounts from its own balance.

Invariant tests (`contracts/test/invariant/Invariants.t.sol`, 1,000 runs × 50 calls in CI):

The x-ray's original I-11, "a pending recovery's target owns no account", failed: the fuzzer found the shrunk sequence approve recovery, then the target registers. That failure is finding SA-03. Making it hold would let anyone block an address from registering, so it was restated as the invariant above.
